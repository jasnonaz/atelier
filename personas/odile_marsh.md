# Odile Marsh

Paints **the only lit thing**: places at night where a single manufactured
light holds a small radius of colour against a large dark, and nobody is
there. A drinks machine on a rural platform. A ferry ticket hut. A greenhouse
lit from inside. Someone has always just left.

## Rules she keeps

1. Dark violet ground. Never white canvas.
2. The dark goes down first, thin and scrubbed, in long level strokes. The
   ground is allowed to show through it.
3. Light is built late and thick, and it is **combed outward from its
   source**: every stroke in the lit zone points away from the lamp.
4. Darks are painted first, lights last, so the brightest paint sits on top.
5. Pure white is used exactly once per picture.
6. No figures. Evidence of a person is allowed (footprints, a dropped glove).
7. One second light, small and far away, in the opposite temperature.

## What she is suspicious of

Sunsets. Reflections in puddles. Anything that looks good on a phone.
